

# Enterprise Knowledge Model (EKM)
**Version:** 1.0 (Draft)

---

# 1. Executive Summary

## 1.1 Purpose

The Enterprise Knowledge Model (EKM) is the authoritative representation of how capital projects are planned, approved, engineered, procured, executed, monitored and governed across the enterprise.

The objective of this document is not merely to document business processes, but to establish a common knowledge model that can be used by:

- Business Users
- Project Teams
- PMO
- Digital & Analytics Teams
- AI Applications
- Knowledge Graphs
- Enterprise Dashboards
- Future Automation Initiatives

This document intentionally separates business concepts from system implementation while preserving the relationships between them.

---

## 1.2 Scope

This document covers the complete lifecycle of a capital project beginning from project initiation through construction progress monitoring.

Domains included:

- Project Initiation
- CAPEX Approval
- Planning
- Pre-Construction
- Engineering
- Procurement
- Construction
- Progress Measurement
- Supporting Governance Domains

The document also establishes the relationships between these domains and the enterprise applications supporting them.

---

## 1.3 Objectives

The Enterprise Knowledge Model aims to:

- Standardize enterprise terminology
- Establish common business entities
- Define relationships between entities
- Capture business rules
- Define ownership
- Identify system of record
- Enable enterprise-wide reasoning
- Support AI-assisted decision making

---

## 1.4 Guiding Principles

### Business First

Business concepts should exist independent of technology.

Systems implement business processes; they do not define them.

---

### Single Source of Truth

Every business object should have one primary system of record.

Derived systems should consume rather than redefine information.

---

### Lifecycle Driven

Every entity participates in one or more lifecycle stages.

Relationships between lifecycle stages are explicitly modeled.

---

### Traceability

Every major decision should be traceable back to its originating business process.

Examples:

- Construction Delay
- Procurement Delay
- Budget Overrun
- Engineering Delay

should all be explainable through upstream business events.

---

# 6. Project Initiation

## 6.1 Purpose

Project Initiation is the process through which a business opportunity is formally transformed into an approved capital investment proposal. The primary outcome of this phase is an approved Capital Expenditure (CAPEX) Note for Approval (NFA), which authorizes the organization to initiate the project.

Project Initiation establishes the business, financial, and execution baseline upon which all downstream activities depend.

---

## 6.2 Objectives

The objectives of Project Initiation are to:

- Identify and justify a capital investment.
- Obtain organizational approval for the investment.
- Establish the approved CAPEX budget.
- Define high-level project scope and objectives.
- Capture the baseline financial assumptions.
- Define the preliminary execution timeline.
- Capture the baseline CAPEX distribution.
- Establish the initial governance for project execution.

---

## 6.3 Process Overview

```text
Business Opportunity
        │
        ▼
Project Proposal
        │
        ▼
CAPEX NFA Preparation
        │
        ▼
Supporting Documents
        │
        ▼
Main Approval Flow
        │
        ├──────────────┐
        ▼              │
Top Approval Flow      │
(If Applicable)        │
        ▼              │
Approval Complete ◄────┘
        │
        ▼
Project Approved
        │
        ▼
Planning Phase
```

---

## 6.4 Business Trigger

The initiation process begins when a business identifies a potential capital investment.

The source of the opportunity varies by Business Unit and may originate from:

- Business Development
- Operations
- Expansion Strategy
- Capacity Enhancement
- Regulatory Requirements
- Technology Upgrade
- Strategic Investment

The identification mechanism is business-specific and is outside the scope of enterprise standardization.

---

## 6.5 Primary Business Artifact

The primary artifact of this phase is the **CAPEX Note for Approval (NFA)**.

The CAPEX NFA is the enterprise's official investment approval document and serves as the governing approval for project initiation.

A project is considered formally approved only after the CAPEX NFA has completed the prescribed approval workflow.

---

## 6.6 Major Information Captured

The CAPEX NFA captures:

### Project Information

- Project Name
- Business Unit
- Project Purpose
- Business Objective
- Project Description
- Project Owner
- Planned Start Date
- Planned Completion Date
- Major Milestones

---

### Financial Information

- Approved CAPEX
- Cash Flow
- Financial IRR
- Debt-Equity Ratio
- Investment Returns
- Other financial indicators applicable to the business

These values establish the initial financial baseline for the project.

---

### CAPEX Planning

The enterprise captures a month-wise CAPEX plan for the complete project duration.

The plan is classified into:

- Hard Cost
- Soft Cost
- Land Cost
- Contingency

This represents the approved CAPEX expenditure timeline.

---

### CAPEX Breakdown

The CAPEX is further decomposed into standardized levels.

Enterprise standardization exists up to Level 3.

Typical hierarchy:

```text
Project
    │
    ├── Hard
    ├── Soft
    ├── Land
    └── Contingency
```

Each category is further divided into enterprise-standard components.

Every CAPEX component maintains:

- Basic Value
- GST
- Refundable GST
- Non-Refundable GST

This structure forms the approved budget baseline.

---

## 6.7 CAPEX vs Cash Flow

The enterprise distinguishes CAPEX from Cash Flow.

### CAPEX

Represents the actual capitalized investment attributable to the project.

Refundable taxes are excluded.

---

### Cash Flow

Represents the total cash movement associated with the project.

Includes:

- Refundable taxes
- Advances
- Actual vendor payments

Although related, these concepts are independently planned and monitored.

---

## 6.8 Pre-Construction Baseline

During project initiation, the initiator also captures the preliminary Pre-Construction Checklist.

Examples include:

- Land Acquisition
- Statutory Approvals
- Utility Availability
- Site Readiness
- Other business-specific readiness activities

For each applicable item, the initiator records:

- Applicability
- Tentative Completion Date

These values establish the initial Pre-Construction baseline and are subsequently consumed by PreCon360.

---

## 6.9 Approval Workflow

The CAPEX NFA progresses through the enterprise approval workflow governed by the **Delegation of Authority (DOA/TOA)**.

The approval path depends on:

- Business Unit
- CAPEX Value
- Approval Matrix

The approval workflow is standardized across the enterprise while allowing business-specific approval chains.

---

## 6.10 Main Approval Flow

Every CAPEX NFA passes through the Main Approval Flow.

This constitutes the mandatory enterprise approval process.

The flow includes multiple approval stages determined by the Delegation of Authority.

---

## 6.11 Top Approval Flow

Projects exceeding defined approval thresholds additionally enter a Top Approval Flow.

This flow supplements, rather than replaces, the Main Approval Flow.

Typical activities include:

- Detailed scrutiny
- Supporting document review
- Executive summary preparation
- Promoter review
- Chairman approval

The Top Approval Flow is coordinated by dedicated governance teams.

---

## 6.12 Supporting Documents

The CAPEX NFA may contain multiple supporting documents.

Examples include:

- Financial Models
- Technical Justification
- Feasibility Reports
- Supporting Calculations
- Other business-specific annexures

Additional supporting documents may be uploaded during the approval process.

Previously submitted documents remain part of the approval history.

---

## 6.13 Project Approval

A project becomes officially approved only after the CAPEX NFA is approved.

Although business teams may begin preliminary preparation while approval is in progress, enterprise governance recognizes the project only after approval.

Historically approved offline projects may exist but are treated as exceptions.

---

## 6.14 Business Rules

### BR-001

One CAPEX NFA generally represents one Project.

Rare enterprise exceptions may combine multiple projects into a single NFA.

---

### BR-002

Every project requires an approved CAPEX NFA.

---

### BR-003

A Project belongs to exactly one owning Business Unit.

Other Business Units may participate as stakeholders but ownership remains unique.

---

### BR-004

Financial metrics approved during initiation become the baseline for future comparison.

Changes occur only through formal amendment processes.

---

### BR-005

Currency for enterprise CAPEX approval is maintained in INR.

Foreign procurements are converted into INR for approval purposes.

---

### BR-006

The Project Manager (or Chief Project Officer, depending on business governance) assumes ownership after project approval.

---

## 6.15 Amendment Process

Approved projects may subsequently undergo amendments.

Typical reasons include:

- Scope Increase
- Budget Enhancement
- Timeline Extension

These are governed through CAPEX Amendment NFAs.

The amendment process establishes a new approval cycle while preserving the original investment approval.

Current enterprise systems do not explicitly maintain parent-child relationships between original and amendment NFAs.

This relationship must be inferred through business context.

---

## 6.16 Outputs

The Project Initiation phase produces:

- Approved Project
- Approved CAPEX
- Financial Baseline
- CAPEX Baseline
- Preliminary Milestones
- Pre-Construction Baseline
- Governance Ownership
- Approved NFA
- Supporting Documentation


# 7. Planning

## 7.1 Purpose

The Planning domain transforms an approved project into an executable schedule. It establishes the project execution strategy, defines the Work Breakdown Structure (WBS), develops the baseline schedule, and creates the activity network against which project execution and progress are monitored.

Planning provides the enterprise schedule baseline and acts as the central coordination layer between Engineering, Procurement, Construction, and Progress Measurement.

---

## 7.2 Objectives

The Planning phase aims to:

- Develop the enterprise project schedule.
- Define the standardized Work Breakdown Structure (WBS).
- Establish activity dependencies.
- Create the approved schedule baseline.
- Identify the Critical Path.
- Assign project resources.
- Enable project monitoring through Primavera.
- Provide schedule data for dashboards and enterprise reporting.

---

## 7.3 Primary System

**Primary System of Record**

- Primavera P6

Supporting Systems

- PreCon360
- Enterprise Data Lake
- Enterprise Dashboards

# 7.5 Schedule Creation

Following project approval, the Scheduler creates the project schedule within Primavera P6.

The schedule serves as the enterprise execution plan and becomes the primary source for monitoring project progress.

Each project maintains a dedicated Primavera schedule.

---

# 7.6 Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)

The enterprise follows a partially standardized WBS.

Standardization exists up to Level 2.

Typical enterprise hierarchy:

```text
Project

│

├── Pre-Construction

├── Engineering

├── Procurement

└── Construction
```

Beyond this level, individual Business Units extend the hierarchy according to project requirements.

Historical SAP structures may not follow this standard; however, all new enterprise projects are expected to comply.

---

# 7.7 Activities

Activities are the smallest operational planning units.

Every activity captures:

- Activity ID
- Activity Name
- Start Date
- Finish Date
- Duration
- Calendar
- Progress %
- Predecessors
- Successors
- Activity Relationships

Activities form the basis of execution, monitoring, productivity measurement, and progress reporting.

---

# 7.8 Calendars

Each activity is assigned a project calendar that determines working and non-working periods.

Calendars influence:

- Duration
- Schedule calculations
- Forecast completion
- Critical Path

---

# 7.9 Activity Logic

Activities are connected using predecessor-successor relationships.

The schedule network determines:

- Execution order
- Critical Path
- Float
- Forecast completion

Activity sequencing is maintained within Primavera.

Business-specific construction and engineering sequences are maintained separately and will be documented as enterprise knowledge assets.

---

# 7.10 Resources

Activities may have multiple assigned resources.

Typical resource categories include:

- Labour
- Machinery
- Materials

Resource assignments support:

- Progress calculation
- Productivity analysis
- Resource planning

---

# 7.11 Progress Calculation

Physical progress is primarily derived from Primavera activities.

Progress is generally calculated using labour-unit based methodologies supported by Primavera.

Certain activities continue to receive manual progress updates where automated measurement is not practical.

---

# 7.12 Baseline

Once planning is completed, the project baseline is established.

Although Primavera supports multiple baselines, the enterprise recognizes one approved baseline as the official reference.

This baseline is used for:

- Executive Reviews
- Dashboards
- Variance Analysis
- Progress Measurement

---

# 7.13 Baseline Approval

The baseline undergoes an approval workflow before becoming active.

Typical approving bodies include:

- Project Management Team
- PMAT (Project Management Assurance Team)
- CPMAT (Central Project Management Assurance Team)

Approval governance varies by business while following enterprise principles.

---

# 7.14 Critical Path

Critical Path is a primary planning construct.

It represents the chain of activities that directly determine project completion.

Management continuously reviews:

- Critical Path
- Total Float
- Free Float

These metrics support schedule risk assessment.

---

# 7.15 Schedule Updates

Schedules are updated periodically throughout project execution.

Typical inputs include:

- Daily Progress Reports (DPR)
- Weekly Progress Reports (WPR)
- Site Updates
- Engineering Progress
- Construction Progress

Update frequency varies by Business Unit, although weekly updates are the enterprise norm.

---

# 7.16 Planning Integration

Planning interacts with multiple downstream domains.

### Pre-Construction

The Pre-Construction Checklist established during Project Initiation is expanded into executable activities.

---

### Engineering

Engineering milestones and package schedules are managed within Primavera.

---

### Procurement

Major procurement milestones are reflected within the project schedule.

---

### Construction

Construction execution follows Primavera activities.

Progress is reported against these activities.

---

### Progress Measurement

Physical progress originates from Primavera activity completion.

Enterprise dashboards consume schedule information from the Data Lake.

---

# 7.17 Dashboards

Planning information feeds enterprise dashboards.

Common planning KPIs include:

- Planned Progress
- Actual Progress
- Schedule Variance
- Critical Path
- Total Float
- Free Float
- S-Curve
- Baseline Variance

---

# 7.18 S-Curve

The S-Curve compares:

- Planned Progress
- Actual Progress

It serves as the primary visualization for monitoring schedule performance against the approved baseline.

---

# 7.19 Business Rules

### BR-007

Every approved project shall maintain a Primavera schedule.

---

### BR-008

One approved baseline shall serve as the enterprise reference schedule.

---

### BR-009

Activities shall maintain logical relationships through predecessor-successor links.

---

### BR-010

Pre-Construction activities shall originate from the approved Pre-Construction Checklist.

---

### BR-011

Activity progress forms the basis for enterprise physical progress reporting.

---

### BR-012

Enterprise WBS standardization is mandatory up to the defined enterprise levels.

Business Units may extend the hierarchy below standardized levels.

---

# 7.20 Outputs

The Planning domain produces:

- Primavera Schedule
- WBS
- Activity Network
- Resource Plan
- Schedule Baseline
- Critical Path
- Float Analysis
- S-Curve
- Planning KPIs

These outputs drive Engineering, Procurement, Construction, and Progress Measurement throughout the project lifecycle.


# 8. Pre-Construction

## 8.1 Purpose

The Pre-Construction domain governs all readiness activities that must be completed before major construction execution can commence. It ensures that statutory, land, utility, regulatory, and organizational prerequisites are tracked, managed, and completed in a controlled manner.

Unlike Planning, which establishes the execution schedule, Pre-Construction focuses on **readiness**.

---

# 8.2 Objectives

The objectives of Pre-Construction are to:

- Track project readiness.
- Monitor statutory approvals.
- Monitor land acquisition.
- Track regulatory dependencies.
- Identify execution bottlenecks before construction.
- Provide enterprise visibility into project readiness.

---

# 8.3 Primary System

**Primary System of Record**

- PreCon360

Supporting Systems

- Primavera P6
- CapExpert
- Autodesk
- Enterprise Data Lake
- Enterprise Dashboards

---

# 8.4 Process Overview

```text
Approved NFA
        │
        ▼
Pre-Construction Baseline
        │
        ▼

        │
        ▼

        │

        │

```

---

# 8.5 Relationship with Project Initiation

During CAPEX approval, the project captures a preliminary Pre-Construction Checklist.

This checklist forms the initial baseline.

After project approval, the baseline is expanded into executable readiness activities within PreCon360.

PreCon360 therefore represents the operational execution of the readiness plan defined during Project Initiation.

---

# 8.6 Standard Checklist

The enterprise maintains a centrally governed library of Pre-Construction activities.

Typical categories include:

- Land Acquisition
- Statutory Approvals
- Utility Readiness
- Environmental Clearances
- Corporate Approvals
- Other business-specific readiness activities

Not every activity applies to every project.

The Business selects applicable activities during project setup.

---

# 8.7 Activity Attributes

Each Pre-Construction activity maintains:

- Activity Name
- Applicability
- Planned Date
- Forecast Date
- Actual Completion Date
- Current Status
- Business POC
- Pending With
- Supporting Documents
- Remarks

Additional metadata is maintained depending on activity type.

---

# 8.8 Land Acquisition

Land Acquisition activities additionally capture:

- Required Land
- Acquired Land
- Balance Land

These values provide enterprise visibility into land readiness.

---

# 8.9 Statutory Approvals

Statutory Approvals are managed as structured approval hierarchies rather than a single checklist item.

Typical hierarchy:

```text
Approval Category
        │
        ▼
Approval
        │
        ▼
Approval Steps
```

Each approval maintains its own lifecycle and progress.

---

# 8.10 Ownership

Every activity has:

### Business Owner

Responsible for overall completion.

---

### Pending With

Represents the team currently expected to take action.

Examples include:

- Legal
- Corporate Affairs
- Land Team
- Other designated internal teams

Only one internal team is considered the active owner at a given time.

---

# 8.11 Status Model

Typical lifecycle:

```text
Not Started
        │
        ▼
In Progress
        │
        ▼
Completed
```

Completion is based on manual confirmation supported by documentary evidence.

---

# 8.12 Supporting Documents

Every activity may contain supporting documents.

Examples include:

- Government approvals
- Land records
- Regulatory correspondence
- Internal approvals

Documents are managed through Autodesk, which serves as the enterprise document management platform.

---

# 8.13 Relationship with Primavera

Pre-Construction activities are mirrored into Primavera.

Planning establishes the schedule.

PreCon360 manages execution-specific metadata not maintained within Primavera.

Examples include:

- Pending Team
- Business Owner
- Forecast Dates
- Risks
- Supporting Documentation

The two systems remain synchronized conceptually while serving different operational purposes.

---

# 8.14 Dashboards

Enterprise dashboards monitor:

- Overall Readiness
- Pending Activities
- Delayed Activities
- Forecast Variance
- Land Acquisition Progress
- Statutory Approval Progress
- Readiness Percentage

These dashboards are consumed during executive project reviews.

---

# 8.15 Completion Criteria

Pre-Construction is considered complete when all applicable readiness activities have been completed.

Activities marked as Not Applicable do not contribute to readiness.

Completion represents organizational readiness rather than physical construction progress.

---

# 8.16 Business Rules

### BR-013

The enterprise checklist is centrally governed.

---

### BR-014

Projects select only applicable activities.

---

### BR-015

Each activity has exactly one current "Pending With" owner.

---

### BR-016

Completion requires manual confirmation supported by documentary evidence.

---

### BR-017

Land Acquisition maintains Required, Acquired and Balance values.

---

### BR-018

Statutory approvals are decomposed into structured approval hierarchies.

---

# 8.17 Outputs

The Pre-Construction domain produces:

- Project Readiness Status
- Land Readiness
- Statutory Readiness
- Regulatory Readiness
- Forecast Dates
- Supporting Documentation
- Readiness KPIs


# 9. Engineering

## 9.1 Purpose

The Engineering domain converts the approved project scope into executable engineering deliverables that enable procurement and construction.

Engineering is a controlled, document-driven lifecycle. Every engineering output progresses through defined review, revision and approval workflows before it becomes available for execution.

The primary objective is to produce technically approved documents that are released for construction.

---

# 9.2 Objectives

Engineering aims to:

- Define technical requirements.
- Develop engineering deliverables.
- Manage engineering vendors and consultants.
- Establish the Master Deliverable List (MDL).
- Control engineering document lifecycle.
- Issue approved drawings for construction.
- Support procurement through technical specifications.
- Support construction through approved engineering documentation.

---

# 9.3 Primary Systems

Primary Systems

- Autodesk CDE / Autodesk Forms
- Autodesk Document Management

Supporting Systems

- Primavera P6
- SAP
- Enterprise Data Lake
- Enterprise Dashboards

---

# 9.4 Engineering Execution Philosophy

Engineering execution follows the enterprise Packaging Strategy.

The Packaging Strategy determines how project scope is divided into executable packages.

Typical package strategies include:

- Engineering
- Engineering + Supply
- Supply
- Supply + Service
- Engineering + Procurement + Construction (EPC)

The Packaging Strategy governs:

- Vendor selection
- Procurement strategy
- Engineering ownership
- Construction responsibility

It is considered a governing business artifact.

---

# 9.5 Engineering Participants

Typical stakeholders include:

### Project Team

Owns package execution.

Coordinates engineering activities.

---

### Engineering Team

Owns engineering execution.

Reviews engineering deliverables.

Coordinates vendors.

---

### Consultant (Owner's Engineer)

May perform:

- Basic Engineering
- Design Review
- Technical Validation
- Vendor Document Review

Some projects may not appoint a consultant.

In such cases, owner engineering performs these activities directly.

---

### Engineering Vendor

Produces engineering deliverables.

Participates in technical clarification meetings.

Submits engineering documents.

Responds to review comments.

---

# 9.8 Building Information Modelling (BIM)

The enterprise adopts BIM principles.

- LOD 100
- LOD 200
- LOD 300
- Higher maturity levels as applicable

LOD primarily applies to engineering models and is not currently used as a formal executive KPI.

# 9.9 Master Deliverable List (MDL)

Following vendor onboarding, a Master Deliverable List (MDL) is prepared.

The MDL defines every engineering deliverable expected for the package.

The MDL is jointly established by:

- Engineering Vendor
- Owner Engineering

It undergoes review until both parties mutually agree on the deliverable baseline.

Once agreed, it becomes the engineering baseline for that package.

---

# 9.10 MDL Attributes

Typical MDL information includes:

- Document Name
- Document Type
- Discipline
- Planned Submission Date
- Planned Approval Date
- Current Status
- Current Revision
- Vendor
- Package
- Priority

The MDL forms the primary planning instrument for engineering deliverables.

Package engineering completion is therefore achieved progressively rather than through a single release event.

# 10. Procurement

## 10.1 Purpose

The Procurement domain transforms approved business requirements into legally awarded contracts for the supply of materials, services and engineering packages.

Although procurement is an enterprise capability supporting both CAPEX and OPEX, this document focuses on procurement activities associated with capital projects.

Procurement begins with an approved Purchase Request (PR) and concludes with the issuance of a Purchase Order (PO) or Service Order (SO).

Material delivery, billing and payment occur after procurement and belong to downstream domains.

---

# 10.2 Objectives

The Procurement domain aims to:

- Procure technically compliant solutions.
- Ensure commercial competitiveness.
- Award contracts to approved vendors.
- Maintain procurement governance.
- Ensure procurement transparency.
- Support engineering and construction execution.

---

# 10.3 Primary Systems

Primary Systems

- SAP
- SAP Ariba
- Ariba BTP (Commercial NFA)

Supporting Systems

- CapExpert
- Primavera
- Enterprise Data Lake
- Enterprise Dashboards

---

# 10.4 Procurement Philosophy

Procurement follows a **Technical → Commercial** evaluation model.

Technical suitability is established before commercial evaluation.

Only technically accepted proposals proceed to commercial comparison.

This separation is a fundamental enterprise governance principle.

---

# 10.5 SAP Project Preparation

Before procurement begins, the project must exist within SAP.

The Capital Finance Monitoring & Control team ensures that:

- Project Definition is created.
- Plant Code is assigned.
- Company Code is assigned.
- SAP WBS hierarchy is created.

The WBS hierarchy is derived from the approved project structure.

Enterprise standardization exists up to Level 3.

Business-specific structures extend beyond this level.

---

# 10.6 Purchase Request (PR)

Procurement begins with creation of a Purchase Request (PR).

The PR represents the formal business request to procure materials or services.

Typical PR information includes:

- Project
- Package
- WBS
- Estimated Budget
- Technical Requirement
- Procurement Category
- Required Delivery

Although multiple packages may technically be included within one PR, enterprise best practice is one package per PR.

---

# 10.7 Budget Validation

Each PR reserves budget against the approved project allocation.

Budget availability is validated before procurement proceeds.

The reserved amount contributes towards project commitment.

(The detailed commitment accounting process is maintained separately within the Finance domain.)

---

# 10.8 Vendor Eligibility

Standard procurement is restricted to enterprise Approved Vendors.

The Approved Vendor Master is centrally governed.

New vendors may undergo qualification separately before participating in procurement.

Vendor qualification itself is outside the scope of the procurement lifecycle.

---

# 10.9 Vendor Platform Onboarding

Vendor qualification and vendor platform onboarding are distinct processes.

Vendor Platform Onboarding grants vendors access to Ariba for participation in procurement events.

This onboarding must be completed before bidding begins.

---

# 10.10 Request for Proposal (RFP)

Each PR results in one Request for Proposal (RFP).

The RFP contains:

- Technical Requirements
- Scope
- Commercial Conditions
- Submission Instructions
- Supporting Documents

For engineering packages, Technical Specifications prepared by Engineering form part of the RFP.

---

# 10.11 Technical Clarification

During bidding, vendors may request clarification.

Typical interactions include:

- Technical Clarification Meetings
- Bid Meetings
- Engineering Discussions
- Consultant Participation

These interactions ensure that vendors understand the technical requirements before bid submission.

Once the clarification stage closes, technical requirements are considered frozen for that procurement event.

---

# 10.12 Technical Bid Evaluation

Following bid submission, Engineering evaluates technical compliance.

The outcome is documented through the Technical Bid Evaluation Report (TBER).

The TBER compares all participating vendors and establishes technical rankings (e.g., T1, T2).

Commercial evaluation begins only after technical evaluation is complete.

---

# 10.13 Commercial Evaluation

Commercial evaluation is performed by Procurement.

Typical activities include:

- Price Comparison
- Reverse Auction
- Commercial Negotiation
- Commercial Deviations
- Final Recommendation

Reverse Auction represents the preferred enterprise mechanism where applicable.

Alternative approaches may be adopted for exceptional procurement scenarios.

---

# 10.14 Internal Estimate

Before commercial evaluation, the Estimation Team prepares an independent cost estimate.

Characteristics:

- Confidential during bidding.
- Visible only to internal stakeholders.
- Included in Commercial NFA.
- Used as a commercial benchmark.

Significant deviation between awarded value and internal estimate triggers additional scrutiny.

---

# 10.15 Commercial NFA

Following commercial evaluation, Procurement prepares a Commercial NFA.

The Commercial NFA captures:

- Procurement Summary
- Vendor Comparison
- Negotiation Outcome
- Internal Estimate
- Recommended Vendor
- Supporting Documents
- Commercial Deviations

Approval follows the Delegation of Authority applicable to commercial approvals.

Large procurements additionally undergo executive scrutiny similar to CAPEX approvals.

---

# 10.16 Purchase Order / Service Order

Following Commercial NFA approval:

- SAP creates the Purchase Order.
- Ariba synchronizes procurement information with SAP.

SAP is the enterprise System of Record for both PR and PO.

Purchase Orders are used for material procurement.

Service Orders are used for service procurement.

---

# 10.17 Purchase Order Contents

Typical Purchase Order information includes:

- Vendor
- Package
- Line Items
- Delivery Schedule
- Payment Terms
- Payment Milestones
- Liquidated Damages (LD)
- Performance Guarantees
- Letter of Credit (where applicable)

The Purchase Order becomes the governing contractual document for execution.

---

# 10.18 Procurement Completion

From a procurement perspective, procurement is considered complete when the Purchase Order or Service Order has been issued.

Subsequent activities such as:

- Delivery
- Inspection
- Goods Receipt
- Invoice
- Payment

belong to downstream operational domains.

---

# 10.19 Purchase Order Amendments

Changes after award are managed through:

- PO Amendment
- Commercial Amendment NFA

Major scope revisions may require Credit Purchase Orders depending upon business requirements.

Current enterprise systems do not explicitly maintain traceable relationships between original Commercial NFAs and amendment NFAs.

These relationships must be inferred through business context.

---

---

---

# 11. Construction

## 11.1 Purpose

The Construction domain governs the physical execution of project work at site. It transforms approved engineering deliverables, procured materials and contracted services into completed physical assets.

Construction is the primary execution domain where engineering, procurement, planning, finance, quality and safety converge.

Unlike Engineering, which is document-centric, Construction is **activity-centric**.

---

# 11.2 Objectives

Construction aims to:

- Execute approved scope.
- Construct assets as per approved engineering.
- Utilize materials efficiently.
- Monitor contractor performance.
- Capture daily execution progress.
- Support physical progress reporting.
- Achieve planned milestones.

---

# 11.3 Primary Participants

Construction execution typically involves:

### Project Team

Overall package ownership.

Responsible for:

- Execution
- Coordination
- Monitoring
- Reporting

---

### Package Owner

Accountable for execution of the assigned package.

Validates progress reported from site.

Coordinates contractors.

---

### Contractor

Responsible for execution of construction work.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Mobilization
- Manpower deployment
- Equipment deployment
- Daily execution
- Progress reporting

Generally, one construction package is awarded to one contractor. However, the same contractor may execute multiple packages across the project.

---

### Site Team

Responsible for:

- Site supervision
- Daily monitoring
- DPR preparation
- Progress validation

---

## 11.4 Construction Readiness

Construction does not begin through a single approval.

Readiness depends upon the activity.

Typical prerequisites include:

- RFC/GFC drawings available
- Relevant statutory approvals completed
- Land available
- Materials available (where applicable)
- Site readiness

Different activities may satisfy different readiness conditions.

---

# 12. Progress Measurement

## 12.1 Purpose

Progress Measurement provides the enterprise with a standardized mechanism to monitor project performance across schedule, physical execution, financial performance and resource utilization.

Unlike Planning, which defines the intended execution path, Progress Measurement evaluates actual performance against approved baselines and forecasts future outcomes.

It is the primary domain used by executive management for project governance.

---

# 12.2 Objectives

The Progress Measurement domain aims to:

- Monitor project execution.
- Compare planned and actual performance.
- Measure physical progress.
- Measure financial progress.
- Monitor productivity.
- Forecast project completion.
- Support executive decision making.

---

# 12.3 Enterprise Performance Dimensions

The enterprise monitors projects through multiple complementary dimensions.

## Physical Progress

Represents actual execution completed on site.

---

## Financial Progress

Represents capital expenditure incurred against approved plans.

---

## Cash Flow

Represents actual cash movement to vendors.

---

## Schedule Performance

Measures execution against the approved schedule baseline.

---

## Productivity

Measures execution efficiency.

---

## Resource Performance

Monitors manpower, materials and machinery.

---

## Financial Metrics

Monitors project investment performance.

---

# 12.4 Primary Systems

Primary Sources

- Primavera P6
- SAP

Supporting Systems

- Enterprise Data Lake
- Enterprise Dashboards
- CapExpert

---

# 12.5 Performance Philosophy

Enterprise performance is always measured against an approved baseline.

Examples include:

- Schedule Baseline
- CAPEX Baseline
- Annual Operating Plan (AOP)
- Financial Baseline

Variances are meaningful only when compared against approved reference values.

---

# 12.6 Physical Progress

Physical Progress represents actual project execution.

The primary source of Physical Progress is Primavera.

Progress originates from validated construction activities and is updated through:

- Daily Progress Reports
- Weekly Progress Reports
- Primavera Updates

Progress is primarily labour-unit based while permitting manual updates for specific activity types.

---

# 12.7 Financial Progress

Financial Progress measures CAPEX incurred relative to approved plans.

The enterprise monitors two complementary views.

### Annual Financial Progress

```text
Actual YTD CAPEX

÷

YTD Approved AOP
```

This is the primary executive KPI.

---

### Overall Financial Progress

```text
Actual Project CAPEX

÷

Approved Project CAPEX
```

This represents total project financial completion.

Both measures are required for executive governance.

---

# 12.8 CAPEX

Enterprise CAPEX is recognized when:

- Material has been received through GRN

OR

- Services have been accepted through SES

Invoice submission and payment are not prerequisites for CAPEX recognition.

This definition differs fundamentally from Cash Flow.

---

# 12.9 Cash Flow

Cash Flow represents actual cash movement.

Typical components include:

- Advance Payments
- Vendor Payments
- Settlement Payments

Cash Flow therefore differs from CAPEX.

A project may incur CAPEX without corresponding cash outflow or vice versa.

---

# 12.10 Annual Operating Plan (AOP)

Every financial year, the enterprise prepares an Annual Operating Plan.

The AOP contains:

- Annual CAPEX Plan
- Annual Cash Flow Plan

The AOP represents only the current financial year and therefore differs from the complete project CAPEX baseline established during project approval.

---

# 12.11 AOP Categories

The annual plan classifies expenditure into:

- Project CAPEX
- Maintenance CAPEX
- Operational Expenditure (OPEX)

This document focuses only on Project CAPEX.

# 17. Enterprise Governance & Business Rules

## 17.1 Purpose

Enterprise Governance establishes the decision-making framework through which capital projects are approved, planned, executed, monitored and controlled.

While individual business domains maintain their own operational workflows, governance provides the common enterprise controls that ensure consistency, accountability and compliance.

This chapter captures governance principles that apply across the complete project lifecycle.

---

# 17.2 Governance Philosophy

The enterprise follows the following governance principles:

- Every project shall have formal approval.
- Every significant business decision shall be traceable.
- Every major business object shall have a defined owner.
- Every lifecycle stage shall have measurable outputs.
- Enterprise standards shall be followed wherever defined.
- Business Units may extend enterprise standards without violating them.

Governance exists to enable controlled execution rather than to restrict business operations.

# 17.10 Governance Exceptions

The enterprise recognizes that certain historical or business-specific scenarios may not fully conform to current standards.

Examples include:

- Legacy projects with offline CAPEX approvals.
- Historical SAP WBS structures not aligned with enterprise standards.
- Exceptional procurement approaches where reverse auctions are not applicable.
- Business-specific package hierarchies.
- Manual operational tracking through spreadsheets where enterprise systems are not yet implemented.

Such exceptions should be documented and governed rather than treated as standard practice.

- AI reasoning shall follow business dependencies rather than application boundaries.
- Every KPI shall be traceable to its originating business objects.
- Root-cause analysis shall traverse upstream dependencies until the originating business event is identified.
- Derived insights shall reference authoritative business data.

These principles ensure that future AI assistants provide explanations consistent with enterprise governance.
